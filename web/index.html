    Builds derive samples and a report from canonical data at one data revision. Pausing stops after the current device; resuming continues from the stored cursor. A new report never overwrites an old one.

    Report history

    Reports are immutable. When data is revised or a late fault arrives, a new build supersedes the old report; the old one stays readable and is marked stale against the current data revision.

    Method, stated plainly

    Three histories, never merged

    • Pre-entry time. Days a device existed before observation began. It is left truncation, not observed fault-free life. The device enters every risk set at its entry age, so early ages are not estimated from late entrants.
    • Not yet failed. Under observation at the cutoff with no fault. Right-censored; future data can still change it.
    • Exited observation. Monitoring was closed before the cutoff without a fault. Right-censored at the exit date, and counted separately from “not yet failed”.

    The estimator

    Kaplan–Meier on the age scale with delayed entry and right censoring; Greenwood variance with a log–log 95% band; a seeded device-level bootstrap as a second band; median and restricted mean reported only to the largest observed age. If the median is not reached, no value is invented.

    Risk sets

    The service clock starts at commissioning (or a repair before observation) and the fraction of the model’s maintenance interval partitions the clock: safe < 0.34, watch 0.34–0.67, danger ≥ 0.67. This is a bookkeeping label supplied by maintenance policy, not a probability and not a prediction.

    Late and revised data

    Faults carry both the date they happened and the date they were reported; a fault reported months late still counts for cuts after its fault date. Corrections and retractions are append-only revisions. Each report records the data revision and algorithm version it used, and is marked stale when the data moves on.

    Cost, not quality

    The two build strategies differ in caching only. One keeps per-device intervals for reuse; the other recomputes everything each time. The comparison table reports measured build time and row counts, never a “better” lifetime.

    What this is not

    No covariates, no hazard model, no extrapolation, no individual diagnosis, no medical or clinical conclusion, and no trained prediction model. The curves describe observed exposure in fictional data; they do not forecast the future life of any device. A naive average over fault records is shown in the side panel only as a contrast, because it omits censored devices and is biased toward short lives.
